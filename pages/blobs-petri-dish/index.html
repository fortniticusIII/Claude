A brain­less blob that builds networks

Why this is remarkable

In 2010, Atsushi Tero and colleagues placed oat flakes on agar in the pattern of cities around Tokyo and let a real Physarum grow between them. Within about a day it built a tube network whose efficiency, cost and fault tolerance were comparable to the actual Tokyo rail system that engineers designed.

No part of the organism sees the whole map. Tubes that carry more flow thicken, tubes that carry little shrink, and a good network falls out of that one local rule. This simulation uses a similar idea from Jeff Jones's 2010 agent model: move forward, sniff three directions, turn toward the strongest scent, leave scent behind.
