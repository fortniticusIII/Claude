Pearson's Zoo

Two chemicals in a dish and one rule. Turn two dials, F and k, and the same rule grows spots, worms, mazes, spirals or nothing at all.

Drag on the dish to drop in more V.

The rule

U is fed into the dish at rate F. Wherever two V meet a U, they turn it into a third V. V is drained away at rate F + k. Both spread out, U twice as fast as V. That is all of it: the Gray-Scott model.

∂u/∂t = Du∇²u − uv² + F(1 − u)
∂v/∂t = Dv∇²v + uv² − (F + k)v

In 1993 John Pearson swept F and k on a computer and sorted what grew into classes he named with Greek letters. Robert Munafo later simulated the whole plane and refined the list. Pick a class below to grow it from Pearson's starting square, or show the whole map: one simulation where k rises left to right and F rises bottom to top, so every class grows in its own neighbourhood at once.
