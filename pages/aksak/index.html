Why the grouping matters

Seven pulses can be counted 2+2+3, 3+2+2 or 2+3+2. The count is the same; the feel is not, because the ear hears each group start as a step and the long group as a stretched one. Turkish musicians call these uneven meters aksak, “limping.”

Two rings at once

Turn on the second ring and give it a different length. Both rings share the pulse but loop on their own, so they drift apart and land together again after the least common multiple of their lengths. Seven against four meets every 28 pulses.

Practicing it

Start slow enough to say the syllables out loud: ta-ka for a two, ta-ki-ta for a three. Then turn off “click every pulse” so only group starts sound. In seven, strict alternate picking puts every other cycle's first note on an upstroke; practice it that way and restarting each cycle on a downstroke.
